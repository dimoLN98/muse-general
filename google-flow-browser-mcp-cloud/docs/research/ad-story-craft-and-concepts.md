## 1. What the research says

### 1.1 Structure of a 10-20 s social ad
* Every source converges on **hook → body (problem/payoff) → close**. TikTok's own version: "hook, body, close"; hook uses suspense, surprise or emotion; "introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and land the hook in the first 6 s; branding in the first seconds "should be subtle and not compromise the hook". [G] https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/creative-codes · https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en · https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf
* UGC-script writers: 0-3 s hook (pattern interrupt: curiosity gap, bold claim, or a **relatable moment**), then the problem made *specific* ("describe the exact moment the problem bites"), then proof by **show, don't tell**, then one CTA. Start mid-action, no "Hi, I'm…" intro, every line speakable in one breath, write 3-5 alternate hooks per script. [C] https://ugcplayground.com/articles/ugc-script-template/ · https://www.retiplex.com/blog/ugc-ad-script-guide · https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-ad-script-structure · https://www.wandura.ai/blog/ugc-ad-script-templates
* Hook testing is the real lever: hold body and CTA constant, swap the first 2-3 s. [C] https://kineclip.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-ugc-ads-2026/ → we should design each concept so the **first beat bar can be swapped** (alt hooks in §4 notes).
* Lookbook/fashion Reels: 7-15 s for outfit drops, clips 1.5-3 s each, on-screen text 3-5 words, CTA saved for the last 2-3 s. [C] https://www.upgrow.com/blog/fashion-lookbook-reel-templates-instagram
* TikTok: sound/music matters ("88% say sound is vital" is a vendor stat [C]); use captions/text overlays for context; transitions and beat-synced cuts hold attention. [G] https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/video-advertising-guide

### 1.2 Skit / dialogue ads and why they convert
* UGC-style content wins because it pattern-matches "a person", not "a commercial"; feeds have trained people to skip anything that looks like an ad. [C] https://reugc.com/blog/ugc-ads · https://storista.io/blog/what-are-ugc-ads
* The **skit** = setup, turn, payoff, product as the resolution; "harder to pull off, but it travels well when it lands, because it feels like entertainment rather than an ad". The problem-agitate-solve skit in 10-20 s is "the format most resistant to skip reflexes and the hardest to execute badly without feeling scripted". [C] https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/ugc-ad-formats-that-convert · https://reugc.com/blog/ugc-ads
* Conclusion for us: a skit only works if the **product is the punchline or the answer**, not a prop. Our print *is* a joke, so the skit's turn can be the print.
* Tension to resolve: TikTok asks for "DIY, not overly polished" [G]; the user's verdict is that Ad 01's quality/resolution was why he liked it [L]. Resolution: keep the cinematography realistic (handheld sway, practical light, real locations, imperfect framing) but **do not degrade resolution**; borrow native *pacing* (mid-action start, beat cuts, captions), not lo-fi image quality.

### 1.3 POV, GRWM, fit check
* Fashion TikTok ads convert at the try-on moment: show the garment on a body in motion, from more than one angle, with a real reaction; GRWM = start-to-finish routine with the product appearing naturally "not as the star"; POV = first-person "POV: you finally found…" frame. [C] https://ugcfast.ai/en/ugc-ads/clothing-tiktok · https://www.inreels.ai/blog/tiktok-ads-that-dont-look-like-ads · https://videoai.me/blog/clothing-brand-ugc-ads-fashion-ecommerce-roas
* Fashion hook wording that reads as organic: a reaction ("Wait.") or a recognition ("POV:…"), not a claim. TikTok's fashion playbook: "fashion flex in the first few seconds", let the content value shine before the brand. [G] https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/ENG_TikTok_Fashion_META_Playbook_New.pdf
* Use: fit-check/GRWM grammar for concept 8; "turn around to show the back" is the fit-check gesture that naturally reveals our back print (concepts 2, 4, 7).

### 1.4 Identity / tribe / niche-story
* Inside-joke apparel works as a **membership badge**: "fans don't line up for a hoodie with a channel name on it, but they will happily buy one that carries an inside joke, a catchphrase, or a moment only true followers understand"; "subtle references, clean typography, inside jokes tend to outperform oversized logos"; "it hints at something and lets the right people connect the dots". [C] https://reveriepage.com/blog/from-fandom-to-fabric-how-creators-and-influencers-can-build-merch-that-turns-followers-into-a-fandom · https://streamhub.world/streamer-blog/strategy/4733-creating-high-converting-merch-stores-for-niche-gaming-communities/ · https://www.jerkyboysstore.com/?s=prank+calls
* IYKYK mechanic: a selective channel that includes some viewers and excludes others; recognising the reference confirms in-group membership (Bourdieu's cultural capital). The "40% more engagement" figure on that page is unsourced; ignore it. [C] https://www.lexistry.com/phrase/iykyk · https://www.boundlessnetwork.com/blog/ethos-the-future-of-sustainable-branded-merchandise
* Streetwear case studies: Corteiz built community before customers (password page, secret drops, "community is the marketing"); Aimé Leon Dore sells one very specific story told consistently; Places+Faces garments "become a uniform… a symbol of belonging". The common thread: **a fixed view of the world and an in-group that recognises each other**. [C] https://growyourclothingbrand.com/blog/case-studies/corteiz/ · https://growyourclothingbrand.com/blog/case-studies/aime-leon-dore/ · https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/36100/community-fashion-places-faces-corteiz · https://itismandystyle.com/blog/underground-streetwear-brands-you-need-to-know-in-2026/
* For Acme: the niche is "people who know what a synapse is". The ad should make that group *feel recognised* (concept 2) and make outsiders feel they just missed an inside joke worth asking about. Never shame outsiders.

### 1.5 Drop-teaser / scarcity (pre-launch)
* Teasers: close-ups, fabric, hints, "don't reveal everything", start 2-4 weeks (or 3-7 days for a single drop) before; early-access lists. [C] https://growyourclothingbrand.com/blog/quick-tips/build-anticipation-before-a-clothing-drop-to-increase-demand/ · https://growyourclothingbrand.com/blog/marketing/social-media-marketing/social-media-content-ideas/
* Honest scarcity only: "consumers are trained to ignore fake urgency". [C] https://festive.clothing/festive-fashion-drops-we-d-actually-queue-for-lessons-from-l → matches our rule: **no fake scarcity, no countdowns we cannot honour**; "Coming soon." is true and enough.

### 1.6 Humour
* Meta-analysis of 369 correlations: humour raises attention, positive affect, attitude to the ad, attitude to the brand and purchase intention, **but lowers source credibility**; the effect on the ad is about twice the effect on the brand. So the laugh does not automatically transfer to the brand: the brand must be inside the joke. [A] https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11747-008-0096-y.pdf
* Humour works better when it is **related to the product/message** (humour-product fit), and weakens the ad if the joke dominates so people remember the gag, not the brand. [A/C] https://www.abacademies.org/articles/the-effectiveness-of-humor-in-advertising-analysis-from-an-international-scope-8419.html · https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mar.21634
* Use: **the print is the punchline** (or the answer). One joke per ad. The name "Acme" and the garment appear *in the joke's resolution*, not after it.

### 1.7 Dialogue that sounds real
* Enter late, leave early: start at the moment it gets interesting, cut greetings and small talk, leave the instant the turn has landed. [C] https://www.thestorydepartment.com/screenwriting-in-late-out-early-2/ · https://screenplaywriter.app/learn/how-to-write-a-scene-screenplay/
* In a short, every line must do two jobs (advance story + reveal character/relationship); on-the-nose lines are "fatal" because there is no runtime to recover; subtext carries weight; silence is emphasis; read it aloud and time it. [C] https://filmmakergenius.com/academy/how-to-write-a-short-film-script/short-film-dialogue
* Real speech: fragments, interruptions, trailing off, distinct voices per person, slang sparingly and age-appropriate. [C] https://writeloom.app/blog/how-to-write-dialogue · https://blog.celtx.com/tips-for-writing-realistic-dialogue/ · https://mywriters.life/guides/screenwriting-tips
* Flow-specific [L]: one line ≈ one word per beat at 138 BPM (≈2.3 words/s); colon-form, no quotes, and **we avoid contractions in the prompt** (earlier finding, `flow-video-ad-craft.md` §2.4). Natural speech normally uses contractions, so all lines below are written as **fragments and imperatives that need none** ("Do not." "Hold this." "Now it does."). If the 360p A/B (OPEN item in `flow-video-ad-craft.md`) shows contractions are safe, loosen.

## 2. Rules we will follow

1. **One joke, and the print is its punchline or its answer.** The viewer must be able to say what the joke was *and* that it was on a hoodie. If the hoodie could be deleted and the story still works, the concept fails.
2. **The product is motivated by the story** (someone reads it, shows it, walks away in it, wears it to do the thing). The back print is revealed because a character *turns, walks, shoots, sits with back to us* for a story reason.
3. **Enter late, leave early.** Frame 1 is already mid-moment (a line, a reaction, an action). No arrivals, greetings, product intros.
4. **Hook in 0-1.7 s (bar 1), proposition by 3 s, payoff by ~10 s, brand by 12 s, end card at 13.9 s.** Acme cues in bar 1 are subtle: badge top, orange rule, one sting. They must not be the hook.
5. **Dialogue: 4-9 words per line, ≈1 word per beat, ≤24 words per ad**, fragments, no ad-speak, no explaining the joke twice, no brand name from AI characters (separate VO says "Acme", EP-suh-lon).
6. **Two jobs per line:** every line moves the story *and* shows who these people are to each other.
7. **Sound is half the ad.** Cut to the 138 BPM grid; at least one diegetic sound becomes part of the rhythm (train chime, dribble, dryer drum, door click). Silence for one beat before the payoff.
8. **Works with sound off:** captions/short text carry the joke; text is 1-5 words; never on a face, never on the garment print; placed in a **measured clean band** (ad 02 lesson: measure on a labelled 10 % grid, confirm by pixel test, check the picture fills the frame after render).
9. **Frame is filled by the subject.** Medium close-ups, over-shoulder, back-of-hoodie fill shots; wide shots only when the wideness is the joke (concepts 5, 6).
10. **Graphics must do something to the garment.** The orange signal travels the real print (plate from the product image, path in image coordinates) or leaps from print to the front neuron-E; it is never decoration.
11. **Honest pre-launch:** CTA is the placeholder "Coming soon." (OPEN-ITEMS #1, remind the user). No scarcity claims, no prices, no AI-persona testimonials, no invented specs. Everything is a staged skit and is disclosed as AI per platform settings.
12. **Max 4 Flow clips; clip lengths 4/6/8/10 s; trim to the beat.** Prefer 4 s clips with one line each; two-line exchanges get 6 s. Never ask one clip for dialogue + move + cut.
13. **Fashion first:** black oversized hoodie shown well (fabric, drape, chest wordmark) in the first half.
14. **Alt hook per concept** (bar 1 swappable) so we can test openers cheaply.

### 5. PASS IT ON (pre-launch teaser · three people · prints as a network)
**Logline:** A whisper travels along a row in a lecture hall. By the time it reaches the third person, nobody knows what they're passing on. Ending: it's the viewer's turn.
**Emotion:** conspiratorial fun, FOMO. Word-of-mouth shown as a literal signal.
**Why:** a teaser is honest ("Coming soon." is the answer to "Pass what on?"), the three backs in a row *are* a neuron chain, and no scarcity claim is needed.

## 6. Top-3 recommendation

The composite puts Field Notes first, but I would **not** lead with it: the user named "real Flow footage of two realistic people talking" as the reason Ad 01 beat Ad 02, and Field Notes has none. It is the best cheap tonal alternative. Recommended order:

1. **CHEAT SHEET** (concept 1). Highest impact: the print is literally the retrieval cue, the joke is one sentence, there are two real people talking, and it reuses the proven pipeline (two characters, plate payoff, orange signal on the real print). Cost ≈ 24 cr first pass.
2. **PASS IT ON** (concept 5). Right for a pre-launch drop: the story *is* word of mouth, the three backs form a neuron chain, and the open CTA is naturally the answer to "Pass what on?" ("Coming soon." in a whisper). Cheapest dialogue ad (≈ 21 cr).
3. **THUMB RULE** (concept 3). Safest to produce: the Ad 01 library setting, two seated people, short lines, and the most relatable joke of the set. Uses the "three pulses, each thicker" graphic that shows the wiring idea in one second.

Hold in reserve: **FIELD NOTES** (run as a tonal A/B at ≈ 21 cr, no lip-sync risk) and **COUNTERSIGN** (highest ceiling for shares and identity, highest production risk; do it after one of the above proves that outdoor two-person scenes hold).

Test plan (from the hook-testing research): produce 1 and 3 with the same end block; for each, keep the alt hook as a second cut of bar 1 only.
